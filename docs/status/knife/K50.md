# K50: achievements (the PS4 trophies)

Package K50, 2026-11-12, version **0.1.9**. No game data, story text, official trophy text, Sony picture or sound was added: the port ships a table of
picture names and squares, and draws its own emblems and padlocks.

## 1. What the player gets

- The PS4's 30 trophies as achievements, earned by the PS4's rules and recorded in `trophies.ini` in the profile, beside `save.dat`. Progress from before
  0.1.9 counts: at start the game records, without a date, what the save already satisfies.
- A notice at the top right of the game (icon, "Achievement unlocked", the name), queued, about 4.6 s each. Off by default: Config > System > Achievement
  Notify, or Notify in the game on the launcher's Achievements page (`trophy_notice`). Achievement Sound, Sound when notified on the launcher
  (`trophy_sound`, off by default), adds a chime and needs the notice on. With notices on, what the start found is shown one notice per achievement on the
  title screen, about 1.5 s each; a key or click the title acts on (PRESS ANY KEY, its cursor, a choice, the quit question) drops the rest.
- An eighth launcher page, Achievements, in both launchers: progress and the four grades, the two switches right under them (card NOTIFY), then tabs All /
  Earned / Locked and one row per achievement.
- The English words say Notify, not Notice (the owner's choice): the launcher's card and switches, Config > System > Achievement Notify and Screenshot
  Notify (Screenshot Notice before 0.1.9); the F12 line of the key tables says "a short message follows", and the comments of `settings.ini`, the guide,
  the game's log lines and the Windows launcher's self-test lines say "message" for what appears and "Notify" for the switch. Settings keys, code names,
  the Japanese and Chinese rows and the notices' own words are unchanged.
- Backups include `trophies.ini`. A restore keeps the record and adds the backup's; an import records what the replaced save implied. Neither touches the
  record while a game runs.

## 2. How it works

- **Rules** (`Tsuki.Engine.Trophies.TrophyRules`, gallery.md 8, script.md 4.1): 1..27 at the `_RDED` of the eboot's 27 trophy scenes outside a replay; 28 from
  `_TRPY(28)` or a lesson started from the ending list; 29 at the `_RDED` of a lesson when all 25 are done, also in a replay; 0 when 1..29 are all earned.
  The same conditions read from a save's scene bits give the catch-up and the launcher's count. Grades: 0 platinum; 14, 26, 27, 29 gold; 1..4 bronze; the
  rest silver. Names: the game's own chapter titles for 1..27; our own words for 0, 28 and 29.
- **Record** (`TrophyLog`): `version=1`, then `<id>=<UTC yyyy-MM-ddTHH:mm:ssZ>` or `<id>=earlier`. Written the moment something is earned; a failed write is
  tried again by the saves at most every 5 s, at once by the next earn and at quit. A file that could not be read is never written over. A file that is not
  a record (a NUL in it, lines of its own and no version or trophy line, over 64 KB) is set aside as `trophies.ini.broken-<UTC time>`. A file that still
  cannot be read just before the start's write may hold the catch-up's achievements already: they are recorded silently, not shown, and the log says why.
  The release rules refuse `trophies.ini`, its temporary copies and `*.broken-*`.
- **Icons** (`Tsuki.Data.Trophies.TrophyIconTable`, `TrophyIconArt`): 30 rows of picture name (or UI sheet child) and square; the square is cut from the
  player's own pictures, reduced by alpha-weighted area average and framed with the grade's ring. Made on a worker when needed (the notice: when earned,
  72 px; the launchers: once the page is first shown, 240 px), kept in memory, never written to disk. A notice waits up to 45 frames for its icon. Without
  one it shows the grade's emblem, a cup drawn by `TrophyIconArt.Emblem`. Locked rows show a padlock tile and never ask for a game picture. Nothing reads
  `sce_sys/trophy/trophy00.trp` any more.
- **Chime** (`AchievementChime`): the owner's recipe made in C# at run time, once, at the mixer's 48 kHz (no audio file in the repository or the release).
  It plays on a mixer voice of its own (0x2B) as a notice comes in, at the Sound Effects volume and 6 dB below it (gain 0.200 at the defaults); it never
  ducks or stops a voice or the music. Silent in skip / fast-forward, with the sound effects muted or at 0, in picture runs and in muted runs (logged
  only); at most one per 1.5 s, one for the whole catch-up. The release rules and the pre-commit hook refuse audio files:
  `.wav .ogg .mp3 .flac .opus .m4a .aac .wma .aif .aiff`.
- **Out of pictures**: the UI marks where the toast and the notice begin (`DrawKind.OverlaysBegin`); the executor keeps the frame as it was at the mark for
  the next transition start, the F12 screenshot and the save thumbnail (compositing.md 6.1).
- **Launchers**: the page reads again when the stamps of `trophies.ini` and `save.dat` change (2 s poll, window activation, the game's end) and follows a
  `settings.ini` the game wrote. Restore and import call `Achievements.KeepBeforeSaveChange` and, for a restore, `UniteBackupRecord`.

Also in 0.1.9: the gallery viewer shows the 11 language-strip pictures in the game's language at the catalog's band stride (English was 190 rows low), and
their thumbnails show the language's frame. `--exit-after N` ends exactly at step N. The Windows launcher's controller list (`Controllers`, K17) reads
Windows' Raw Input device list instead of the winmm joystick API, which ran the controller driver's code in the launcher: a fault there closed it at start.
A pad is a HID collection of usage page 1 with usage 4, 5 or 8 (XInput pads too); a device's collections of different kinds are one pad, of the same kind
one pad each (vJoy, multi-player adapters). Its name is the registry's OEMName, else the HID product string, else "Game controller". The list is read
beside the refresh, never with the game folder: a refresh waits for it at most 2 s at its end and applies a later list when it comes, a refresh that finds a
probe running takes the last list, and a probe that is slow, never answers or throws costs only the names on the Controls page (one `launcher.log` line).

## 5. Known and open

- The .NET suites ran on Windows and in WSL (Ubuntu, headless). Nothing was run on a real Linux or macOS desktop or a Steam Deck; the Avalonia launcher was
  tested headless only.
- D11 to D13 ran as Debug builds on Windows (suites and real runs) and in WSL as published test suites and the Linux release package; nothing of them
  ran on macOS or on a hosted runner before the push, and no Release build of the game or of the Windows launcher was started with them.
- The chime was never heard through a sound device (every run was muted): it is checked by its samples and its mix.
- No real window was shown: the game in hidden runs, the launchers through `--render` and `--selftest`. A launcher page following a running game was not seen.

Found by the re-review and its checkers and not changed in 0.1.9 (D11 to D13):

- With Achievement Notify on, an F12 picture taken in the first frames after a flowchart jump can show the achievement message: the clean copy of the
  frame is put back only before a frame that captures. A save thumbnail made in those frames was not tested.
- A fade may start from an older picture when the loop takes two steps in one pass (a visible window slower than the game's steps). Older than 0.1.9,
  from reading only: hidden runs draw every step and cannot show it.
- A launcher switch flipped while the game runs is read at the game's next start, as every launcher setting is. A Config switch that cannot be saved
  (`settings.ini` read-only or held) works until the game ends and is off again at the next start, with a log line only.
- A `trophies.ini` another program replaces while the game runs (a sync tool) is written over at the next earn.
- `save.dat` held without sharing by another program at the instant of a start ends that start with the error box; the next start works.
- Windows, saves: a commit while both `save.dat` and `save.dat.bak` are open elsewhere for more than 0.2 s still fails once, as before (nothing is
  damaged, the next commit works). A `save.dat.bak` that can never be replaced (read-only, held for good) stays at its old generation while saves go on,
  each 0.2 s slower, and no log line says so. A profile left with only `save.dat.bak`, or with a commit's temporary file but no `save.dat.bak`, starts
  a new profile, and that profile's second save replaces `save.dat.bak`. The launchers do not finish a cut-off commit: after one they show no save, and Back up now does not copy its files, until the game has
  been started once: after a crash during a save, press Play once before restoring or importing.
- Saves: stray `save.dat.<8 hex>.tmp` files (a game killed during a save leaves them) are never removed. A reset by hand removes `save.dat`,
  `save.dat.bak` and `save.dat.*.tmp`; a whole stray newer than the backup that is left brings its save back once. The start-up recovery trusts file
  times: with a clock set back, an older stray can be taken, and a backup dated after the cut-off file means a new profile. A newest candidate that
  another program holds or that cannot be read stops the recovery, and that start begins a new profile, as before 0.1.9.
- On Linux a named pipe called `save.dat`, `trophies.ini` or (beside `save.dat.bak`) `save.dat.<8 hex>.tmp` makes the start wait for ever; only a
  hand-made file can be one.
- A game folder whose `eboot.bin` is not a SELF ends the start with the error box and `crash.log` instead of the folder refusal.
- On Linux (and likely macOS) a backup that fails part way through a file (a full disk) can leave a partial backup in the Saves list, as in 0.1.8; a
  restore backs up the current save first.
- The Windows launcher's controller list: a pad slow to name itself is named late, and after the game the window comes back up to 2 s later; two
  refreshes that meet within microseconds can leave the older list on show until the next one. The names of real pads are untested; the game finds pads
  through SDL as in 0.1.8.
